title: "Religious Legacies and Gender Equality in Indigenous Governance: Evidence from Mexico (with Elena Barham and Edgar Franco-Vivanco)"
collection: publications
status: "Working Papers"
abstract: "Indigenous governance institutions shape the lives of approximately 477 million Indigenous people globally and regulate nearly 
50\% of the world’s land. While their importance is widely acknowledged, significant gaps remain in our understanding of the internal variation 
across these institutions—particularly regarding the criteria for access to positions of authority. Who is recognized as an authority within these 
communities, and how is that status defined and contested? This paper investigates the diversity of access to authority within Indigenous governance 
structures in Mexico, with a specific focus on gender. Drawing on novel community-level data from the Instituto Nacional de los Pueblos Indígenas, 
we document variation in women’s access to positions of authority within their communities. We advance a two-step argument to explain this variation: 
first, we suggest that foundational institution-building in Indigenous communities under the Spanish Crown was fundamentally shaped by proximity to 
Catholic missions, which reshaped gender norms through a combination of gendered assimilation and patriarchal teachings. Second, institutional changes 
over time layered on top of variation in local gender norms, leading to the formalization of gender inequality in access to positions of authority. We 
test this theory using our cross-sectional data to provide evidence of missions as a driver of gendered inequality in access to positions of authority, 
with differences concentrated in authorities created after the colonial period."
