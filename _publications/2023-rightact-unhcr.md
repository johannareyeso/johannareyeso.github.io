---
title: "Who Reaches Geneva? Civil Society Repression and the Displacement of Human Rights Advocacy (with Leonardo Arriola)"
collection: publications
status: "Working Papers"
abstract: "How does civil society repression affect the information about human rights violations that reaches the international community? We argue that repression reshapes transnational advocacy by changing who supplies information about rights violations. Repression alters the composition of advocacy by raising the costs of domestic participation, creating space increasingly occupied by international organizations. We examine these expectations through the Universal Periodic Review (UPR) of the United Nations Human Rights Council. Using a large language model to identify participating organizations and their rights-related submissions, we analyze a corpus of more than 10,000 stakeholder submissions filed from 2008 to 2022. We find that rising civil society repression is associated with fewer domestic submissions to the UPR and less coalition formation among the domestic organizations making those submissions. Notably, this decline is concentrated among less politically threatening rights domains, while advocacy for civil and political rights remains relatively resilient. Repression changes the composition of UPR advocacy as international coalitions increasingly substitute for domestic coalitions. Repression thus systematically changes the rights-related claims that reach international audiences by affecting who is able to make them."
